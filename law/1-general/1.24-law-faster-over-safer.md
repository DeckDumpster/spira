# §1.24 law-faster-over-safer

Every decision in the system biases toward faster over safer. When speed and caution conflict, take the faster path and make it safe by being cheap to undo — a revert, a rollback, an eject — not by adding steps, checks or waits before acting. A slow, many-step path is itself a safety risk: every extra step is another place to fail, and a stalled loop invites bypasses, so speed reduces the risk of breaches rather than adding to it. Caution that costs hours is the wrong trade unless the action cannot be undone (destructive action on production data stays Ryan's).
