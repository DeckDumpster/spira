A periodic job does only the work that changed since its last run, never a full redo of everything on each tick: back up new chunks, not the whole store; rescan what moved, not the whole tree. A big-bang job turns a steady trickle into a burst that saturates a disk or CPU at intervals, makes throughput unpredictable for everything sharing the host, and grows until it breaks a limit. Scar: the bd off-box backup re-uploaded the whole 5 GB store every 6 h, took ~25 CPU-minutes per run, outgrew S3's 5 GiB single PUT and was killed by its unit timeout (sp-8btkwq).
